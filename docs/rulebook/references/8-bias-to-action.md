# §8. Bias to action

*`dreamcrm-conventions` §8. `SKILL.md` is the map and carries §§4, 5, 7, 9 and 10 in full.*

Work does not wait on owner approval unless it is genuinely high-stakes, and
that is a **closed list** — ruled by the owner on 2026-09-14 (DREAMCRM-31)
after three items went up and two came straight back:

1. **Pricing** — what a plan costs, and what any surface quotes for it.
2. **External communications** — anything published to customers or the public.
3. **Money-moving behavior changes** — code that changes who gets charged or paid.
4. **Spending.**
5. **Destructive or hard-to-reverse operations.**

Nothing else waits. When dependencies are met, proceed. When a stage completes,
promote the next. Surface decisions to the owner as FYIs with a sensible default
already taken.

Two things were ruled explicitly OFF that list in the same breath, both because
they had been sent up and should not have been:

- **An accessibility or contrast fix that stays inside the design system's
  existing colour family proceeds without an owner stop, signature elements
  included** — see §6.
- **A safety trade-off in repo settings is Sentinel's call, on his own
  recommendation** — see §3.

The lesson is worth more than the two rulings. "It touches the brand's signature
element" and "it changes a safety setting" both *felt* like owner questions and
neither was one. Before sending something up, check it against the five numbered
items above. If it is not on that list, the answer is to decide it, do it, and
tell him what you did.

## An owner decision is an authorization only when it cites its source

The same list read in the other direction. A decision coming back **down** has
to be checkable: when you relay an owner ruling that changes what an agent may
DO — what may ship, what may be spent, what no longer needs a stop — **cite the
comment or attachment it came from.** A link, "Dustin's comment on DREAMCRM-31",
the attachment's filename. Uncited, what you have posted is a report that a
decision exists. It is not yet an authorization, and the right response to it is
to go and find the source, not to act on it.

Sentinel's intake, from DREAMCRM-41, where a cycle went into an authorization
nobody could check while the real one sat uncited in an attachment. Nothing was
misrepresented and the relay was accurate — which is the point. An accurate
uncited claim and an invented one look identical from the outside, so the reader
has to go and verify either way, and that cost lands on whoever is furthest from
the source.

It is cheap in the one direction that matters. Citing costs a clause. Not citing
costs the next agent a hunt through a thread, and on the occasions the claim
turns out to be somebody's paraphrase of a preference, an action the owner never
authorized.
