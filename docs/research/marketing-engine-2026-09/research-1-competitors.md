# How the dental front-office incumbents acquire customers

Research memo for the DreamCRM marketing engine — 2026-09-09.
Scope: 24 named competitors plus the 1,000+-practice players discovered along the way.
Method: ~65 web searches and ~60 page fetches (SEC filings, earnings transcripts, vendor pricing/legal/partner pages, review sites, community sponsor pages, trade press). Every figure carries a source URL. Where a number is my own arithmetic or a reading between the lines it is tagged **[inference]**; everything else is **[sourced]** unless marked **[vendor claim]** (a number the vendor states about itself that nobody has audited) or **[third-party estimate]** (Latka/Tracxn/review-site figures).

Limits of this pass: paid-search ad copy could not be observed directly (no ad-library or SEMrush access), so paid-search claims rest on the vendors' own disclosures and on the competitor-pricing SEO ecosystem that is visible in results. Dentaltown sponsorship data was not retrievable. Several vendor pages (Adit pricing, Practice by Numbers pricing, AADOM 2025 exhibitor list, Zocdoc pricing) sat behind bot-verification walls; third-party write-ups were used instead and are labelled as such.

## Part 1 — Per-competitor profiles

### 1. Weave (NYSE: WEAV) — the benchmark for what "large-scale" costs

**Scale [sourced].** 2025 revenue $239.0M (+17% YoY); 39,625 customer locations at 12/31/2025 after 4,628 net new locations in the year; "more than 30,000 customers" across the US and Canada; GAAP gross margin 72.1%; net loss $28.1M; free cash flow $12.9M; 2026 guidance $273–276M. Gross revenue retention 89%, dollar-based net retention 93%. ([SEC 8-K earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609151/000160915126000005/a991-weaveearningsreleasex.htm); [10-K summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/WEAV/10-k-weave-communications-inc-files-annual-report-237a68ecdf5f.html)). Q2 2026 revenue $67.5M (+15.5%). Dental is the core vertical but "declining as a percentage of overall revenue" as specialty medical (athenahealth marketplace, 160k providers) accelerates ([Q3 2025 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/23/weave-weav-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript/); [Q2 2026 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/weave-weav-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).

**What acquisition costs them [sourced + inference].** GAAP sales & marketing was $102.7M in 2025 (43% of revenue), $24.3M / 40% of revenue in Q3 2025, $25.8M / 38% in Q2 2026. **[inference]** $102.7M ÷ 4,628 net-new locations = ~$22k of S&M per *net* new location. With 89% gross retention on a ~35k base, roughly 3,850 locations churned, so gross adds were ~8,500 and S&M per *gross* add is ~$12k. Average revenue per location is roughly $239M ÷ ~37k average locations ≈ $6.4k/yr (~$540/mo); at 72% gross margin that is a ~2.6-year CAC payback on gross adds. This is the cost structure of a demo-gated inside-sales motion selling phones + software, and it is the number a self-serve challenger is competing against.

**Channels they demonstrably use [sourced, from the 10-K].** "Subscriptions are primarily sold through our direct inside sales team," organised by motion (inbound, outbound, upsell, mid-market) and by vertical. Marketing: "paid and organic social media, paid and organic search, digital advertising, third-party industry advertising and sponsorships, direct mail, and digital events." A business-development team manages "technology integration partners, key-opinion leaders, IT-installers, buying groups, affiliates, and distributors" who "refer customers to us on a commissioned basis." They also "generate demand and sell directly to healthcare practices at trade shows." A majority of customers pay monthly, a "significant number" annually. In Q1 2026 they verticalised inbound sellers and moved prospecting from AEs to an SDR team — and the transition caused "booking shortfalls in May, June, and July" (Q2 2026 call). Management's stated thesis: PMS/EHR integrations are the acquisition engine — "integration rollouts have driven 2x to 5x year-over-year sales growth" within 12 months of launch, and integrated customers retain better (Q3 2025 call).

**Association endorsement [sourced].** In March 2026 Weave became the *exclusive* ADA Member Advantage–endorsed patient-engagement platform; members get "discounts and enhanced training" after ADA "conducted a vetting process" ([ADA News](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2026/march/ada-member-advantage-endorses-weave-as-patient-engagement-platform/); [Weave IR](https://investors.getweave.com/news/news-details/2026/Weave-Named-Exclusive-Patient-Engagement-Platform-Endorsed-for-American-Dental-Association-Members/default.aspx)). By August it "has become the second most productive affiliate program since its introduction" (Q2 2026 call). State associations relay it ([WVDA](https://wvdental.org/2026/05/weave-patient-engagement-platform/)). Royalty terms to the ADA are not disclosed.

**Referral program [sourced].** $50 when a referral completes a demo, $500 when they become a customer ([getweave.com/refer](https://www.getweave.com/refer/)). Legal terms: run on PartnerStack (PayPal/Stripe/direct deposit); US/Canada; referred business must not be a current or 2-year-former subscriber, in an active sales process, or have "received a demonstration ... within thirty (30) days"; must sign within 90 days of demo; payment "within thirty (30) days of the referred business' ninetieth (90) day of active Weave service" ([referral terms](https://www.getweave.com/legal/referral-terms/)).

**Community/buying-group deals [sourced].** Dental Nachos lists Weave at "$50 Amazon Gift Card" for booking a demo ([Nachos tech deals](https://www.dentalnachos.com/technology-sponsors-deals)). Nifty Thrifty Dentists offers members "no activation fee, free phones, $549 per month, $600 off when paying 12 months upfront" ([Nifty Thrifty Weave page](https://niftythriftydentists.com/weave/), figures from search snippet; page did not render). AADOM 2025 conference sponsor and "WILD About AADOM" 20th-anniversary party sponsor; 2026 sponsor again ([AADOM 2025](https://2025.aadomconference.com/sponsors/); [AADOM 2026](https://exhibitor.aadomconference.com/sponsors/)).

**Content [sourced].** "The Happy Practice Playbook" podcast (launched 2022) with dental KOL guests ([press release PDF](https://s21.q4cdn.com/507868397/files/doc_news/Weave-Launches-The-Happy-Practice-Playbook-Podcast-to-Drive-Insights-to-Dental-Offices-07-07-2022-2022.pdf)); YouTube channel; a large "how to" SEO blog (e.g., a patient-referral-program guide that ranks for referral queries — [getweave.com/patient-referral-program](https://www.getweave.com/patient-referral-program/)).

**Review sites [sourced].** Capterra: 673 reviews, 4.3/5, listed starting price $199/mo per location, "no free trial, no free version"; top cons are unreliable phones, slow support, glitches ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/141842/Weave/)). "Leader" in 34 G2 categories (Summer 2025). Trustpilot and BBB carry cancellation complaints — a 1-year contract with a $3,500 early-termination demand, a "cancelled cancellation," a free-trial-turned-$2,700-charge ([BBB complaints](https://www.bbb.org/us/ut/lehi/profile/project-management-software/weave-1126-90025478/complaints?page=2); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/getweave.com)).

**Pricing/trial motion [sourced].** Demo-gated; no free trial; base plan $199/mo advertised, but third-party breakdowns put real configurations at $348–$497+ with bundles ("Coordinate" and "Attract" at $149 each) and a ~$750 setup fee ([Emitrr's Weave-pricing page](https://emitrr.com/blog/weave-pricing/); [PracticeSignal](https://practicesignal.com/dental/compare/weave-vs-nexhealth-vs-solutionreach)). Free VoIP phones with contract are the classic hook.

**Surprising/clever.** (a) The ADA exclusive is a paid affiliate program wearing an endorsement's clothes, and it became their #2 affiliate channel in five months. (b) They quantify PMS integrations as a *sales* channel, not a feature, and co-sell with the EMR vendor on large deals. (c) They pay $50 just for a demo and let community sponsors re-sell the same $50 gift card as a "member deal."

**Exploitable weaknesses.** 89% gross retention means ~1 in 9 locations leaves each year; phones are the #1 complaint; contract/cancellation grievances are public on BBB; real price is 2–3x the headline; 40% of revenue goes to selling, which a $200/mo product cannot copy and should not try to.

### 2. NexHealth — the platform we ride, and a competitor

**Scale.** $177.2M raised across 8 rounds; $125M Series C in 2022 at a $1B valuation ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/nexhealth-hits-unicorn-status-125m-series-c-patient-experience-platform); [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/nexhealth/__XhmhFSh6uRXSjuSt3yUjTyqquxDhPj_NErlr3O2FezE)). 2023 ARR $47.1M and headcount down from 298 (2023) to 217 (Nov 2025) **[third-party estimate, Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/nexhealth.com)** — a signal of post-2022 belt-tightening. "10,000+ dental and medical practices" **[vendor claim]** on the partner page ([partnerships](https://www.nexhealth.com/about/partnerships)).

**Pricing/trial.** Custom quote; third parties cite ~$350 per feature/module per month, modular (Scheduling, Forms, Communications, Payments, Verification, Insights), Starter/Standard/Pro bundles ([Molar Report](https://www.themolarreport.com/learn/nexhealth-pricing); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/182271/NexHealth/pricing/)). Terms are unusually clean for the category: month-to-month or annual, "no cancellation fees," 90 days' notice on annual, and — important — "We'll buy out your contract with your existing vendor" and "Practices don't pay for the Synchronizer ... no setup fee, no subscription" ([pricing](https://www.nexhealth.com/pricing); [FAQ](https://www.nexhealth.com/frequently-asked-questions)). No free trial; demo-gated.

**Referral program [sourced].** $25 per qualified referral + $500 when the practice signs (the $500 only to *current* customers); paid by check ~4 weeks post-launch + 1 month; unlimited ([referral page](https://www.nexhealth.com/referral/submit-a-referral)).

**Partner program [sourced].** Open to "technology companies, marketing agencies, consultants, practice advisors"; self-signed referral agreement, co-branded kit, portal, and *recurring* commission "when deals close" (percentage undisclosed) ([partnerships](https://www.nexhealth.com/about/partnerships)). Evidence of it working: Identity Dental Marketing hosts a NexHealth referral form ([identitydental.com/nexhealth](https://identitydental.com/nexhealth/)); North Digital signed a two-way referral agreement ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/north-digital-inc-nexhealth-sign-133500599.html)); Reach partnership ([nexhealth.com](https://www.nexhealth.com/resources/reach-partnership)).

**SEO footprint [sourced].** A dedicated compare hub with pages against Tebra, Zocdoc, Weave, Solutionreach, LocalMed, RevenueWell, Lighthouse 360 and Simplifeye ([compare](https://www.nexhealth.com/resource/compare)); monthly product-update posts; a how-to library. The developer program (Synchronizer API, developers.nexhealth.com) is itself a channel: every vendor that builds on it — DreamCRM included — becomes a distribution surface for the "Book Now" widget.

**Review sites.** G2 ~103 reviews at 4.8 ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/nexhealth/reviews)).

**Product-led.** The "Book Now" widget is placed on websites, social profiles and Google Business Profiles and writes back to the PMS ([online booking](https://www.nexhealth.com/features/nexhealth-online-booking)) — the widget's own branding on patient surfaces is the loop. **[inference]** Hosted booking pages carry NexHealth branding, but I could not verify a "Powered by" badge from the pages fetched.

**Weaknesses.** "The most expensive option by monthly cost" per an affiliate comparison site ([PracticeSignal](https://practicesignal.com/dental/compare/weave-vs-nexhealth-vs-solutionreach)); no published prices; modular pricing stacks quickly ($350 × N); implementation $500–3,000 and data migration $300–2,000 cited by third parties; headcount shrinkage suggests the growth-at-all-costs era is over.

### 3. RevenueWell — the Patterson channel play

**Scale.** 13,000+ practices at the Feb 2021 PBHS acquisition ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/revenuewell-completes-acquisition-of-leading-dental-digital-marketing-company-pbhs-867918445.html)); the vendor now claims "11,200+" per review.dental **[vendor claim, unverified]** ([review.dental](https://review.dental/reviews/revenuewell)). Owned by Marlin Equity; PBHS (7,000+ dental websites, [pbhs.com](https://www.pbhs.com/dental-website-design/)) is the agency/website arm; May 2025 Mila Health AI-assistant partnership ([revenuewell.com](https://www.revenuewell.com/article/revenuewell-partners-with-mila-health-to-launch-next-gen-ai-powered-assistant)).

**The channel that matters.** Patterson Dental lists RevenueWell on its own site as the patient-engagement layer alongside Eaglesoft, with Patterson software sales (866-590-3384) and support numbers on the page ([Patterson](https://www.pattersondental.com/cp/software/patient-engagement/revenuewell)); review.dental describes it as "distributed through Patterson Dental channel" with "Patterson's Technology Center" handling training. This is the distributor-rep motion: the equipment rep who already visits the office sells the subscription.

**Pricing/contracts.** Quote-only; third parties cite ~$189/mo starting, RW Virtual Office $149/mo, "contract lock-in and undisclosed add-ons" as the recurring complaint ([review.dental](https://review.dental/reviews/revenuewell); [Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/emr-software/revenuewell)). Capterra 81 reviews, 4.5 ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/160904/RevenueWell/reviews/)). AADOM 2025 and 2026 sponsor.

**Weaknesses.** Long-term contracts, hidden add-ons, a "clunky" messaging UI, one reviewer's "zero measurable improvement in recall or production after a full year" — and total dependence on the Patterson relationship.

### 4. Solutionreach — the cautionary tale

**Scale.** Founded 2000; "over 25,000 customers ... one billion messages a year to 80 million patients" **[vendor claim]** (search summary of [Crunchbase/PitchBook profiles](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/solutionreach)); Summit Partners growth investment (dated May 2012 on Summit's site — [Summit](https://www.summitpartners.com/news/solutionreach-announces-growth-equity-investment-from-summit-partners); a 2024 date surfaced in one snippet is unverified).

**Pricing/contracts [third-party].** $400–800/mo for a 3–8-provider practice, $1,000–2,500 multi-location; annual contract required; $500–2,500 implementation ([noshowcost.com](https://noshowcost.com/tools/solutionreach-pricing)). Annual auto-renewal with a 15-day cancellation window, "not BBB accredited" with unanswered complaints ([PracticeSignal](https://practicesignal.com/dental/compare/weave-vs-nexhealth-vs-solutionreach)); BBB filings describe continued billing after disconnection and "we do not prorate" ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ut/lehi/profile/computer-hardware/solutionreach-inc-1166-22310766/complaints)). Capterra 236 reviews, G2 167 ([G2 seller](https://www.g2.com/sellers/solutionreach)).

**Channels.** AADOM 2026 sponsor; long-running SEO content library; demo-gated. Nothing observable that is clever.

**Weaknesses (the whole profile is one).** Auto-renew traps, cancellation friction, "mature but not modern" UI, variable support — the reviews read like an ad for a month-to-month challenger.

### 17. Emitrr — the SEO-attack playbook

### 2b. What the matrix says

1. **Every incumbent is demo-gated and quote-priced.** Of 24, only Podium (list prices), Flex (a range), PbN (Core tier only), Emitrr and the two marketplaces publish a number. Not one dental patient-engagement vendor offers a genuine self-serve signup with a card-free trial. Every "Try for Free" button I followed led to a "quick consultation." This is the single largest open lane.

2. **The bribe-for-a-demo is standard.** Weave, Adit and Dental Intelligence pay $50 Amazon gift cards for a demo; Opencare pays $100 in Uber Eats; DI waives $1,500 of hardware/setup; Curve gives three months free. The category has trained dentists to expect a gift for their time. **[inference]** A $50 gift card at a ~20–30% demo-to-close rate is a $170–250 CAC contribution — trivial next to the inside-sales cost that follows.

3. **Referral cash is remarkably uniform: $500 for a closed practice.** Weave ($50 + $500), NexHealth ($25 + $500), Swell ($300), Birdeye (plan-dependent Amazon card). Payout is slow (Weave: 30 days after day 90; NexHealth: ~2 months) and restricted to current customers.

4. **Partner programs are where the recurring money is.** NexHealth pays recurring commission to consultants/agencies with a self-signed agreement; Podium runs both 30% rev-share and CPL tracks on Impact; Birdeye has 750 agencies reselling with no white-label; Weave's 10-K names KOLs, IT installers, buying groups and distributors as commissioned referrers; DI certifies coaches. The dental consultant is the highest-leverage human in this market: she runs 20–60 practices' front offices by proxy.

5. **The distributor rep is the incumbents' moat.** Patterson sells RevenueWell; Henry Schein sells Dentrix/Ascend/Lighthouse/Sesame; Straumann fronts CareStack; the ADA now fronts Weave. Comparable channels are closed to a $200/mo product (the rep needs a $6k+/yr ticket to care) — but they are also the reason incumbents cost 2–3x.

6. **Community "deals" are a paid media channel, not community.** Dental Nachos lists 20 tech sponsors and 12 marketing sponsors with codified member deals ([tech](https://www.dentalnachos.com/technology-sponsors-deals); [marketing](https://www.dentalnachos.com/marketing-resource-deals)); Nifty Thrifty codifies a Weave price. The going rate is one month free / waived setup / a gift card. This is bookable by anyone.

7. **Comparison SEO is now a three-layer ecosystem**: vendor "X vs Y" pages (NexHealth, Adit, PbN), challenger "X pricing exposed" pages (Emitrr), and affiliate comparison sites that monetise clicks (PracticeSignal, The Molar Report, review.dental, noshowcost.com, SoftwareFinder). The affiliate layer will list a product that pays a commission and has a public price.

8. **Review sites are a scale signal, and dental-only vendors are thin there.** Birdeye ~4,000 / Podium ~2,000 G2 reviews vs Weave 673, Adit 298, Solutionreach 236, RevenueWell 81, DI 53, NexHealth ~103. A dental-only challenger can reach the top three on Capterra "dental" with a few hundred reviews.

9. **Retention is the incumbents' quiet problem.** Weave's 89% gross retention is the only audited number, and every review corpus is loud about auto-renewals, non-prorated invoices, early-termination fees and mid-term price increases (Solutionreach, Weave, DI, Curve). The category's NPS is being spent on contracts.

10. **The PMS integration is both moat and channel.** Weave attributes 2–5x sales growth to each new integration and co-sells with EMRs; Flex lives inside Open Dental's ecosystem; HSO's API Exchange gives 140 vendors "marketing tools." Riding NexHealth's Synchronizer makes us integrated on day one — and also makes NexHealth a channel to be listed in, and a competitor whose booking widget we are effectively distributing.

## Part 3 — What a $200/mo transparent, self-serve challenger should copy, adapt, and avoid

### Copy outright (cheap, proven, nobody in dental does it self-serve)

1. **Publish the price and make the whole funnel self-serve.** Every incumbent hides price; every affiliate comparison site and every "X pricing" article exists *because* they hide it. A public $200/mo with a card-free 7-day full trial is the differentiator and should be the headline of every page, every listicle pitch and every community deal. It also qualifies us for the affiliate/comparison layer automatically.

2. **Run the $500-per-closed-practice referral program, but pay faster and pay everyone.** Match the number the market already knows ($500), pay at day 30 not day 90, pay by Stripe/ACH not check, and open it to non-customers (consultants, hygienists, office managers) — the incumbents restrict the $500 to current customers, which caps the program at their base.

3. **Build the consultant/agency partner track on NexHealth's shape**: self-signed agreement, co-branded kit, portal, *recurring* commission (name it — 20% for 12 months beats Podium's 30% for one year in practice because our price is public and the practice keeps it). Reach: AADOM chapter leaders, dental coaches, IT installers, dental-specific bookkeepers.

4. **Be on the Dental Nachos / Nifty Thrifty deal pages.** The going deal is one month free or a waived setup fee; ours can be "first month free + no contract, ever," which is a better deal than Weave's $50 card and costs $200 in foregone revenue.

5. **Publish 8–12 "X vs DreamCRM" and "X pricing (2026)" pages** aimed exactly where the incumbents are weakest: Solutionreach auto-renewal, Weave's real price and phone complaints, DI's $500 setup + 12-month term, Kleer's percentage fee, NexHealth's $350/module stack, Curve's mid-term increase. Cite BBB/Capterra, not adjectives. (Emitrr's playbook, with our public price as the payoff.)

6. **Farm Capterra/G2 reviews deliberately**, in-product after a win ("first review request sent," "first booking") — 300 dental reviews puts us above every dental-only incumbent except Adit.

7. **Use the marketplaces' product-led idea in our own patient surfaces.** Zocdoc, Opencare, NexHealth's Book-Now widget and Kleer's plan pages all recruit the *next* practice from the patient-facing surface. Our "powered by" credit on every clinic site, booking page, review landing and membership page is that loop and should carry a specific offer, not just a logo.

8. **Do the ADA move at the state and study-club level.** Weave's ADA exclusive is a paid affiliate program; state associations relay it. We cannot buy national exclusivity, but a state-association or study-club "member rate" ($150 instead of $200) with a rev-share back to the association is the same mechanic at our scale.

### Adapt (right idea, wrong cost shape)

- **Content.** DI's podcast + annual "State of Dentistry" report and Planet DDS's "Industry Outlook" work because they produce citable numbers. We have the same telemetry (booking latency, review-request conversion, recall reactivation) across clinics; a quarterly one-page benchmark beats a weekly podcast nobody staffs.
- **Trade shows.** Weave and RevenueWell buy AADOM booths; our version is the office-manager who already uses us presenting at her AADOM chapter, plus a $500 referral for each attendee who signs.
- **Demo incentive.** Do not bribe for demos; there is no demo. Bribe for *going live* instead ("$50 to the front desk when your first review request goes out") — same $50, paid for activation, not attention.
- **Free tools.** None of the incumbents runs a genuinely useful free tool (BoomCloud and Weave run calculators; Kleer's plan builder is inside the product). The grader (docs/marketing-engine.md) is already the right shape — it should also emit the numbers the "X pricing" pages cite.

### Avoid

- **An inside-sales team.** Weave spends 40% of revenue on S&M and pays ~$12k per gross new location for a ~$540/mo ticket. At $200/mo the arithmetic forbids it; the only motions that pencil are self-serve + partner + product-led.
- **Contracts, setup fees, auto-renewals, non-prorated exits, mid-term price increases.** Each one is a named complaint against a named incumbent. "Cancel from Settings, prorated to the day" belongs on the pricing page.
- **Hardware as a hook.** Free phones create the lock-in the reviews hate and the support load that sinks Weave's rating.
- **Generalist positioning.** Podium/Birdeye/Tebra win reviews at scale but lose on dental depth; our copy should never sound like theirs.
- **Distributor dependence.** RevenueWell's fortunes are Patterson's; a channel we cannot get is also a channel we cannot lose.
- **Percentage-of-revenue pricing** (Kleer, Zocdoc, Opencare) — it is the one thing dentists complain about more than contracts.
