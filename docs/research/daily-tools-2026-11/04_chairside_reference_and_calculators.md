# Chairside reference tools, calculators, timers and patient-education tools (US dental, as of Nov 2026)

Research note: coverage is partial (about 15 searches/fetches). Many questions could not be answered with citable data; those are in the Gaps lists. Facts only; no clinical advice.

## Drug and safety references (Lexicomp/Lexidrug, Epocrates, Medscape, dental drug apps, anesthetic and prophylaxis lookups)

### Takeaway
The dental-specific paid reference is Wolters Kluwer's UpToDate Lexidrug for Dentistry (formerly Lexicomp for Dentistry), with no public price. The visible low-cost chairside option is the Apps 4 Dentist "Dental Drugs" iOS app (4.7 stars, about 1.2K ratings, $2.99/yr subscription, built-in anesthetic calculator). Several free or academic anesthetic calculators also exist.

### Cited Findings
- Lexidrug for Dentistry (formerly Lexicomp for Dentistry) is sold by subscription with dental-specific monograph fields, interaction/allergy/dose screening, and a companion mobile app; no price is shown on the vendor page. — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/uptodate/pro/lexidrug-for-dentistry)
- Related price reference points only: UpToDate professional subscription was listed at $579/yr, trainee $219/yr (not Lexidrug for Dentistry). — [Wolters Kluwer UpToDate Pro](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/uptodate/pro)
- Dental Drugs (Apps 4 Dentist): rating 4.7/5 from about 1.2K ratings; free with in-app purchase; 1-week trial then yearly subscription listed at $2.99 (yearly subscription introduced in v1.037, May 2024); 90+ dental drugs with liver/kidney/pregnancy info; anesthetic dose calculator (lb/kg; 1.8 and 2.2 mL cartridges); emergency and treatment protocols; email drug info to patients; offline; English and Spanish; v3.2.0 recently updated (actively maintained). Requires iOS 18+. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dental-drugs/id1010795610)
- A search summary of that app's reviews mentioned a broken protocol button and an Rx-screen crash (single-reviewer reports, not verified against the live reviews). — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dental-drugs/id1010795610)
- DentalRx (free, 60+ drug monographs) exists on iOS; update cadence not stated. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6471300758)
- Epocrates, Medscape, UpToDate and Drugs.com are listed as general prescribing apps in a 2018 AAFP comparison; I found no evidence of dental-specific Epocrates content. Medscape had ~5M Google Play downloads in a 2023 survey of dental-related Play apps. — [AAFP FPM 2018](https://aafp.org/pubs/fpm/issues/2018/1100/p11.pdf); [Eur J Gen Dent 2023](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-0043-1761931.pdf)
- Local-anesthetic dose tools that exist: MaxSafe (free, open-source progressive web app; cartridge-based input, cumulative fractional toxicity, epinephrine tracking; authors say no previously free peer-reviewed tool combined these); LoAD Calc (mobile app, described in a 2021 paper); a published dental nomogram (Walker et al.); SafeLocal (Johns Hopkins, general not dental, comorbidity adjustment); an older "Local Anesthesia" app reviewed by iMedicalApps in 2013 (current availability unclear). — [MaxSafe paper](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202617532508900.page); [iMedicalApps 2013](https://www.imedicalapps.com/2013/03/local-anesthesia-app-guidance-safe-doses/); [SafeLocal](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1440999841)
- The hand arithmetic dentists/hygienists do: mg per cartridge = concentration % x carpules x 18 (1.8 mL cartridge; 4% articaine cartridge is 1.7 mL), then compare to a mg/kg maximum. — [Today's RDH](https://www.todaysrdh.com/hygiene-clinician-and-mathematician-three-local-anesthetic-calculations-explained-and-simplified/)
- Maximum-dose values vary by source, with published reviews of "maximum recommended doses". — [PubMed 30275134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30275134/); [Decisions in Dentistry](https://decisionsindentistry.com/article/update-on-maximum-local-anesthesia-dosages/)
- Antibiotic prophylaxis: clinicians are pointed to ADA Oral Health Topics pages; the ADA defers to the 2021 AHA statement for infective endocarditis (small subset of patients; clindamycin no longer recommended as alternative); the ADA 2015 prosthetic-joint guidance advises against routine prophylaxis. — [ADA](https://www.ada.org/en/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/antibiotic-prophylaxis)

### Inferences
- A cartridge-based anesthetic calculator is the most clearly unmet-by-hand job: academics built several, and the paid-app leader embeds one.
- The ADA/AHA prophylaxis rules changed enough (clindamycin dropped, joints) that stale cached references are a content-maintenance risk for any vendor.

### Gaps
- No usage frequency data (how often dentists look up drugs or use calculators), no "most looked-up" data.
- No Lexidrug for Dentistry price; Epocrates/Medscape dental-specific usage not found.
- Pediatric weight-based dosing calculators: no specific rated app found.
- Anticoagulant management and medical-emergency protocol lookups: no sources found beyond the emergency protocols inside Dental Drugs.
- Google Play ratings and review counts not retrieved.

## Other clinical calculators and references (tooth numbering, perio staging, CAMBRA, ortho, ASA, radiograph, coding)

### Takeaway
Dental-specific calculator apps are small, mostly free and academic; the evidence of heavy real-world use is thin. Only a few were found (PerioCalc, DentiCalc).

### Cited Findings
- PerioCalc (Rutgers): free iOS/iPadOS/Mac perio staging and grading calculator (peri-implantitis too, based on the 2018 classification); the developer labels it educational and not for patient care. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6470758932)
- DentiCalc: ~100,000 Google Play downloads in a 2023 survey of 206 dental apps; the survey concluded engagement with dental m-health apps is relatively low. A blog calls it a "4 in 1" dentist app (functions unconfirmed). — [Eur J Gen Dent 2023](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-0043-1761931.pdf)
- A conservative dentistry/endodontics app review found only 37 qualifying apps, 81% fully free. — [Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40496-022-00325-w)
- A 2023 scoping review of 21 studies of dental smartphone apps focused mostly on patient-facing uses such as anxiety and monitoring. — [PubMed 37886928](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37886928/)
- Roundups (DentistryIQ, Dentaltown, DentalPost etc.) are marketing-style lists from 2012-2024 naming Dental Dictionary, Oxford Dentistry, Dental Anatomy, iRomexis; weak evidence of real use. — [DentistryIQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/practice-management/practice-management-tips/article/16363589/the-10-most-useful-apps-for-dentists)

### Inferences
- Tooth-numbering conversion, eruption charts, ASA, Bolton/Moyers, CAMBRA, coding helpers are implied by the question but I found no app, rating, or usage data for them.
- Abandonment: no confirmed abandoned apps identified; the 2013 "Local Anesthesia" app is old and unverified.

### Gaps
- No Reddit/Dentaltown threads were retrievable via search (results were blog lists); no forum recommendation data.
- No evidence on CAMBRA, Bolton/Moyers, implant/endo, radiograph-selection, shade or coding apps.

## Chairside patient education (CAESY, Consult-PRO, BiteFX, Spear, Dear Doctor, DDS GP, ToothIQ) and case acceptance

### Takeaway
Consult-PRO's price appears as $99-$150/month on aggregator sites (not vendor-confirmed); CAESY Cloud is a Patterson product. Case-acceptance claims are vendor-adjacent anecdotes, not controlled evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Consult-PRO: $99/month on Software Advice, TrustRadius, G2; $150/month on GetApp/Capterra; Capterra US says "contact vendor"; 900+ presentations; email or text videos to patients. — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/consult-pro-patient-education/pricing); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/consult-pro-patient-education/); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/dental/consult-pro-suite-profile/)
- Patterson case study reports a practice using CAESY Cloud with a patient-education plan reached "as much as a 90 percent acceptance rate" (vendor case study). — [Patterson](https://www.pattersondental.com/media/default/pdfs/caesy_casestudy.pdf)
- Dental Products Report articles argue education drives acceptance (trade press). — [DPR](https://dentalproductsreport.com/why-patient-education-crucial-case-acceptance)
- DDS GP is described in a roundup as showing patients how dental issues affect their mouths over time. — [Delmain blog](https://delmain.co/blog/top-dental-apps/)

### Inferences
- Patient-ed is a paid-subscription category with entrenched vendors; a toolbox would more likely link or embed than compete on content library.

### Gaps
- No pricing found for CAESY, BiteFX, Spear, Dear Doctor, ToothIQ; BiteFX not found at all.
- No independent data on actual usage frequency or controlled case-acceptance effect.

## Post-operative instruction delivery

### Takeaway
Research suggests delivery mode affects retention, but no study cleanly compares paper, text, email and portal.

### Cited Findings
- Fixed-prosthodontics study: delivery technique (verbal, video, written) affected understanding and one-week retention. — [PMC10329542](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10329542/)
- Minor oral surgery pilot (n=90) comparing verbal, pamphlet, and app (ExoDont): written 75.3% vs verbal 71.3% compliance, not significant; phone follow-up scored higher. — [PMC9277528](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9277528/)
- Small pediatric study: post-op appointment attendance 60% with texts (n=11) vs 80% with phone calls (n=10), baseline 43%. — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7800v0fz)

### Inferences
- Evidence is small-sample; no basis to claim text beats paper.

### Gaps
- Clinician wishes/pain points about post-op delivery: no sources found.

## Regulatory and liability posture

### Takeaway
FDA's Jan 2026 CDS guidance supports treating clinician-facing, transparent calculators as non-device CDS, with caution on time-critical outputs. MDCalc positions itself as not a certified medical device with professional-use terms.

### Cited Findings
- FDA's final CDS guidance (Jan 2026, reissued Jan 29, 2026) supersedes 2022; revised mainly as clarification; HCP-facing only; silent on AI. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance)
- Non-device test (FDCA 520(o)(1)(E)): no image/signal analysis; displays/analyzes medical info; supports HCP decisions; HCP can independently review the basis. Single recommendations can now qualify; time-critical recommendations remain a concern (addressed under criteria 3 and 4, with automation-bias rationale). — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-20-automation-bias-and-clinical-practice-fda-makes-incremental-updates-to-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance)
- Mobile Medical Apps policy (Sept 2022 version): enforcement discretion for many minimal-risk functions; oversight focuses on device functions that could pose risk if they fail. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/policy-device-software-functions-and-mobile-medical-applications)
- MDCalc: terms say tools are point-of-care CDS for healthcare professionals; users acknowledge outputs are "not certified as a medical device"; no medical advice given; disclaimer effective 3 July 2025, terms 20 May 2026. A third-party guide (CASRAI) says MDCalc aligns with Cures Act non-device CDS (not confirmed in MDCalc's own text). — [MDCalc disclaimer](https://www.mdcalc.com/disclaimer); [MDCalc terms](https://www.mdcalc.com/terms-and-conditions)
- Dental tools' own posture: PerioCalc says educational, not for patient care; calculators carry "user is responsible" disclaimers. — [PerioCalc](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6470758932)

### Inferences
- Design choices consistent with the non-device criteria: show formula, inputs, and source citations so the clinician can review the basis; professional-only framing; avoid urgent auto-directives.
- Nothing here is a legal opinion; a patient-facing version would fall outside the CDS guidance (HCP-facing only).

### Gaps
- I did not read the full FDA guidance text or MDCalc's complete terms; no dental-specific FDA enforcement examples; state-law and malpractice-liability posture not researched.
