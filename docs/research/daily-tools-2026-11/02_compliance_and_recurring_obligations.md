# Compliance, licensing, credentialing and equipment-upkeep obligations of a US dental practice (2026)

NOTE TO WRITER: This pass was limited (~16 tool calls, mostly search summaries). Sourced facts are in "Cited Findings". Items I believe from background knowledge but did NOT verify are listed under "Gaps" as UNVERIFIED so they are not mistaken for findings. Federal / CDC-recommendation / state layers are labelled.

## Infection control (spore testing, logs, CDC vs state)

### Takeaway
CDC (a recommendation, not law) says spore-test every sterilizer at least weekly, plus mechanical and chemical monitoring every load, and keep records for as long as state/local rules require. Waterline testing has no single CDC interval (follow the unit manufacturer). State mandates layer on top and were not individually verified.

### Cited Findings
- CDC (recommendation): use a biological indicator (spore test) "at least weekly" to monitor sterilizers, with a control from the same lot; also required for every load containing an implantable device (implant not used until negative). Part-time sterilizers get no separate rule. — [CDC dental IPC FAQ](https://www.cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/dental-ipc-faqs/sterilization-monitoring.html)
- Mechanical (gauges/printout) checked every load; chemical indicator inside every package. Chemical indicators do not replace weekly spore tests. — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/dental-ipc-faqs/sterilization-monitoring.html); [CDC summary](https://cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/summary/sterilization-disinfection.html)
- Keep mechanical, chemical and biological records for as long as state and local regulations require (CDC defers retention length to the state). — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/dental-ipc-faqs/sterilization-monitoring.html)
- Positive spore test: remove sterilizer from service, review procedure, repeat spore test immediately; if repeat negative and procedure correct, return to service; if repeat positive, keep out of service until repaired and passes three consecutive fully loaded cycles; recall implantables, recall/reprocess suspect loads back to last negative test where possible. — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/dental-ipc-faqs/sterilization-monitoring.html)
- Waterlines: CDC and ADA both use <500 CFU/mL for water in nonsurgical dental procedures; CDC sets no fixed testing interval and points to the dental-unit manufacturer's schedule. A manufacturer (Hu-Friedy) FAQ describes a step-down schedule from monthly testing to no less than every three months after consecutive passes; 500-2,000 CFU/mL triggers review and retest. — [ADA DUWL](https://www.ada.org/en/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/dental-unit-waterlines); [Hu-Friedy FAQ](https://www.hufriedygroup.com/documents/40060)
- Indiana University offers a mail-in sterilization monitoring service (an example of a university spore-testing service dentists use). — [IU Dentistry](https://dentistry.iu.edu/professionals/sterilization-monitoring-service/index.html)

### Inferences
- "Weekly" is the unambiguous, high-value recurring task; it is tied to each sterilizer (per-device, not per-practice), and a positive result spawns an event-driven workflow (retest, quarantine, recall), not just a reminder.
- Waterline cadence is per-device and manufacturer-dependent, so a product must let the practice set it rather than hard-code it.

### Gaps
- Not verified: OSAP positions; whether specific states (e.g. mandate weekly testing, log retention years) differ; eyewash weekly activation (OSHA/ANSI Z358.1 says weekly for plumbed units) and surface disinfection/instrument-processing log expectations; waterline "shock" cadence. UNVERIFIED background: ANSI Z358.1 weekly eyewash activation; many state boards adopt CDC by reference.

## OSHA and HIPAA

### Takeaway
OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens standard (29 CFR 1910.1030) drives annual training, annual exposure control plan (ECP) update, a sharps injury log and hepatitis B records; it is reportedly the top reason dental offices are inspected. Penalties are standard OSHA maxima, not dental-specific.

### Cited Findings
- Employers must establish and annually update an ECP and provide initial and annual training covering all standard elements. — [OSHA OSHA-3186](https://osha.gov/Publications/osha3186.pdf) and secondary summaries (search result set: [OSHA BBP eTool](https://obis.osha.gov/SLTC/etools/hospital/hazards/bbp/bbp.html))
- Sharps Injury Log required under 1910.1030(h)(5)(i) (subject to Part 1904 exemption); hepatitis B declination form is mandatory Appendix A; hep B series must be offered within 10 days of assignment. — [OSHA BBP standards page (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120927234404/http:/www.osha.gov/SLTC/bloodbornepathogens/standards.html)
- 2025 federal OSHA maxima: $16,550 per serious/other-than-serious/posting violation; $165,514 willful/repeated; $16,550/day failure to abate; serious minimum $1,221. A Jan 2026 article repeated the same numbers; I could not confirm a 2026 adjustment. — [AAOMS OSHA summary](https://aaoms.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025_MayJune_OSHA_standards.pdf); [360training](https://www.360training.com/blog/osha-civil-penalties-increase-what-you-need-know)
- Failure to follow the BBP standard is described as usually the No. 1 reason dental practices get OSHA inspections/violations. — [Dentistry IQ](https://www.dentistryiq.com/practice-management/practice-management-tips/article/16352672/workplace-safety-osha-is-watching-dental-practices) (trade press; claim is a characterization)
- Real citations: a Texas dentist fined $5,900 for lack of timely staff training; an Illinois dental practice got six serious and one other-than-serious violations, $9,500 proposed; a Massachusetts practice cited for respiratory protection; a DC dental company cited for BBP hazards. — [Dentaltown](https://www.dentaltown.com/blog/post/8610/osha-fines-north-tx-dentist-5900-for-lack-of-timely-staff-training); [Dr Bicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-hygiene/infection-control/article/15377074/osha-cites-mass-dental-practice-for-respiratory-protection-violations); [EHS Today](https://www.ehstoday.com/osha/dc-dental-company-cited-osha-bloodborne-pathogen-hazards); [DOL release](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/osha/osha20160705) (Illinois case per search summary, not opened)
- Several states run their own OSHA plans (states fact sheets appear in results) — not verified per state.

### Inferences
- Per-employee annual training dates and an annual ECP review are cleanly schedulable; hep B declinations and new-hire (within 10 days) tasks are event-triggered.

### Gaps
- Not found: HIPAA training and Security Risk Analysis cadence (UNVERIFIED background: HIPAA Security Rule requires periodic risk analysis, widely read as annual; training "periodically"; no fixed federal interval); hazard communication/SDS upkeep cadence (29 CFR 1910.1200; training at hire and on new hazards, SDS kept current); sharps log retention (UNVERIFIED: 5 years); dental-specific OSHA citation statistics (no dataset found).

## People: licenses, CE, CPR, DEA, credentialing

### Takeaway
State-by-state variance is large (cycles from 2 to 3 years, 24 to 63+ hours, differing mandatory topics). Federal/payer items (DEA 3-year registration, one-time MATE 8-hour training, CAQH 120-day re-attestation, ~3-year payer recredentialing) recur on separate clocks per person.

### Cited Findings
- California: dentist needs 50 CE units for renewal (generally 1 unit = 1 hour); RDH/RDHEF 25 units on a 2-year cycle, RDHAP 35. — [CA Dental Board](https://www.dbc.ca.gov/licensees/dentist_continuing_education.shtml); [CA DHBC](https://www.dhbc.ca.gov/licensees/licenses_renewal.shtml)
- New York: dentists 60 contact hours per 3-year registration period (NYSED); separate infection control and barrier precautions coursework, reported as every four years (once every other triennial period); one-time child-abuse reporting course. Note: one source adds an "annual update of office practices" that I did not find in NYSED text. NY hygienist figure (24 hours/3 years) is from a 2017 chart only. — [NYSED dentists CE Q&A](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/dentists/continuing-education-q-a); [NYSED infection control](https://www.op.nysed.gov/about/training-continuing-education/infection-control-and-barrier-precautions); [Dentaltown chart](https://www.dentaltown.com/images/DentalTown/magimages/0117/cePg94.pdf)
- Texas: hygienists 24 hours every 2 years; extra 12 hours in-person (3+ clinical simulation) for hygienists using lasers; Texas board also audits CE attestations. Dentist hour total not confirmed in my results. — [TSBDE hygienist CE](https://tsbde.texas.gov/licensing__trashed/hygienists__trashed/hygienist-continuing-education); [TSBDE dentist CE](https://tsbde.texas.gov/licensing/dentists/dentist-continuing-education)
- Florida: hygienist renewal 24 hours per statute 466.014 (2006 version in results; current number not confirmed), first renewal requires 2 hours HIV/AIDS; current cycle referenced to Feb 28, 2028. Dentist total not confirmed. — [FL statute 466.014 (2006)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2006/466.014); [FL Board hygienist renewal](https://floridasdentistry.gov/dental-hygienist-renewal/)
- Other states (concrete variance): Nebraska 30 hrs/24 months; Kentucky 30 hrs/2 years; Delaware 50 hrs/2 years; Washington 63 hrs/3 years. — [PA DOS 50-state comparison](https://www.dos.pa.gov:443/ProfessionalLicensing/Documents/50-State-Licensing-Comparison/Dentistry.pdf) and state pages in results (e.g. [Delaware code](https://regulations.justia.com/states/delaware/title-24/department-of-state/division-of-professional-regulation/1100/section-1100-6-0)); ADHA hygienist-by-state chart: [ADHA](https://www.adha.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/7512_CE_Requirements_by_State_UPDATED.pdf) (2022; not opened)
- DEA: registration valid 3 years; MATE Act requires one-time 8 hours on controlled-substance prescribing/OUD, due at first registration or renewal on/after June 27, 2023; dentists within 5 years of graduating deemed compliant. Not a recurring federal requirement. NYSDA describes a recurring state-level schedule for New York (within one year of DEA registration, then every three years) — conflict between NY and federal reading; verify. — [AGD](https://agd.org/constituent/news/2023/04/03/new-law-requires-8-hours-of-training-as-part-of-dea-registration); [ADA DEA](https://www.ada.org/en/resources/practice/legal-and-regulatory/dea-registration); [NYSDA](https://www.nysdental.org/practice-support/new-federal-mandate-(mate))
- CAQH ProView: re-attestation every 120 days (180 in Illinois per a Maryland reference); plans can only see data once re-attested; payer recredentialing about every 3 years (payer policy, varies); missing re-attestation can lead to inability to recredential and contract termination. — [ADA credentialing](https://www.ada.org/credentialing); [Anthem dental](https://providernews.anthem.com/dentalbc/articles/credentialing-with-caqh-proview-23976); [Priority Health](https://www.priorityhealth.com/provider/manual/standards/credentialing/recredentialing); [Maryland quick ref](https://health.maryland.gov/pophealth/Documents/Local%20Health%20Department%20Billing%20Manual/LHD%20Billing%20Manual%20V32/Section%20VII%20Cred%20and%20Contract/CAQH%20Proview%20PR%20QuickRef.v2.pdf)

### Inferences
- One person carries 5-8 independent clocks (license, CE, CPR, DEA, state CS registration, CAQH, payer recred, malpractice), each with state-dependent length: a rules database keyed by state + role is the hard part.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Florida and Texas dentist CE totals; dentist CE for NY hygienists today; CPR/BLS cycle (UNVERIFIED: AHA BLS 2 years; many boards require current CPR); state controlled-substance registration and PDMP duties; radiography certification, nitrous/sedation permit cycles; NPI (UNVERIFIED: no renewal, only update within 30 days of changes); malpractice renewal (annual, UNVERIFIED); board discipline for lapsed license/missed CE (no examples found).

## Equipment and facility

### Takeaway
X-ray inspection intervals vary by state radiation program (found: TX 4 yrs for dental, VA 3, UT 5 with annual tube registration, MI up to 5). EPA's amalgam rule is a one-time report plus ongoing separator maintenance and record-keeping.

### Cited Findings
- Texas dental x-ray inspection interval 4 years. — [Texas DSHS](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/es/node/27631)
- Virginia: inspected within 3 years; Utah: 5 years, tubes registered annually; Michigan: not to exceed 5 years; CDA page says registration fees per radiation source every two years (state not specified, likely California — unconfirmed). — [VDH](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/radiological-health/radiological-health/x-ray-machine-program/dental-x-ray-machines/); [Utah DEQ](https://deq.utah.gov/waste-management-and-radiation-control/inspections-registrations-and-fees-x-ray-program); [Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/leo/0,5863,7-336-78421_11407_35791-371919--,00.html); [CDA](https://www.cda.org/?p=47502)
- EPA dental effluent rule (40 CFR Part 441): one-time compliance report (existing offices due Oct 12, 2020; new sources within 90 days of first discharge); new report when separator replaced or ownership transfers (per state forms); separators inspected per manufacturer instructions; records kept at least 3 years including amalgam pickup/shipment dates (441.50(b)). Applicable to offices that place or remove amalgam; "annual inspection" term not confirmed. — [EPA dental effluent](https://www.epa.gov/eg/dental-effluent-guidelines); [Tennessee](https://tennessee.gov/environment/permit-permits/water-permits1/npdes-permits1/npdes-pretreatment-program/dental-rule.html); [Michigan EGLE](https://www.michigan.gov/egle/about/Organization/Water-Resources/industrial-pretreatment/epa-dental-rule); [Mississippi MDEQ](https://www.mdeq.ms.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Dental-Office-Rule-2018-brochure.pdf)

### Inferences
- Local control authority (the city/POTW) administers the EPA rule, adding more variance; ownership change as a trigger is a good event-based reminder.

### Gaps
- No sources obtained for: CA, FL, NY x-ray cycles; dosimetry badge cycles (UNVERIFIED: monthly/quarterly vendor swaps); autoclave, compressor, vacuum maintenance; fire extinguisher (UNVERIFIED: annual professional, monthly visual per NFPA 10/OSHA 1910.157); AED; emergency drug expiry and oxygen; nitrous system checks.
