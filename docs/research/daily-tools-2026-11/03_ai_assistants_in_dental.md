# AI assistants in US dental offices (late 2026): products, usage, demand for voice, legal boundaries

Research note: built from about 15 searches/fetches on 2026-11-26. Many price figures are third-party listings and conflict. Forum verbatims, funding data, state dental board positions and CDT royalty amounts were NOT found (see Gaps).

## 1. Dedicated products: voice perio, scribes, narratives, receptionists, knowledge assistants, smart speakers

### Takeaway
The voice and scribe category is real and has measurable traction (Bola AI and Denti.AI claim 10,000+ users each). Pricing is mostly quote-only, with public figures roughly $25-$400 per user or location per month. AI phone receptionists are a crowded, mostly blog-reviewed market with $50-$800/month price points. I found no dedicated dental "knowledge assistant" chatbot and no current smart-speaker product beyond DEXVoice.

### Cited Findings
- Denti.AI Scribe is $299 per location per month or $129 per single user per month. Its integrations include Dentrix, Dentrix Ascend, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Denticon, CareStack, Curve and others, and it supports 50+ languages. Vendor claims: 19 DSO customers across 2,886+ locations, 10K+ dental professionals, up to two hours saved daily. This is a vendor-authored comparison and is self-interested. — [Denti.AI blog, Feb 2026 data](https://www.denti.ai/blog-posts/best-dental-ai-scribes-in-2026-a-comprehensive-review)
- Denti.AI Voice Auto-Chart extracts findings, maps them to teeth and surfaces, and assigns CDT codes; Voice Perio pricing is not stated. — [Denti.AI blog](https://www.denti.ai/blog-posts/best-dental-ai-scribes-in-2026-a-comprehensive-review)
- A third-party comparison lists a Denti.AI Scribe + Voice Perio bundle at $399/month. Another listing gives $49-$99+/month, so the figures conflict. — [Capterra/aggregator search results](https://www.capterra.com/p/10015733/Denti-AI-Voice/)
- Bola AI (voice perio plus scribe) does not publish pricing and is quote-only. Claims: 10,000+ active users, 3 million+ charts, 99% voice accuracy, a Heartland Dental case study with 9x ROI (vendor claims). It integrates with Dentrix, Dentrix Ascend, Curve, Patterson and Open Dental. — [Denti.AI comparison](https://www.denti.ai/blog-posts/best-dental-ai-scribes-in-2026-a-comprehensive-review); [Capterra Bola AI](https://www.capterra.com/p/10003273/Bola-AI/); [Open Dental page](https://opendental.com/site/bolaai.html)
- A Dentrix help article reports Bola saving 3-5 minutes per perio chart in a case study with a large DSO. — [Dentrix Bola FAQ](https://intercom.help/dentrix---bola-voice/en/articles/8839983-bola-voice-faq-dentrix)
- Other scribes:
  - OneChart.ai: Free (40 notes/month), Plus $79/user/month, Pro $125/user/month; includes a CDT/CPT/ICD-10 coding agent.
  - Kiroku: about $25/user/month; UK-founded; includes AI note auditing plus referral and insurance narrative generation.
  - Dentinote: from $49/month.
  - Heidi Health: free tier, Pro $99/provider/month; deep CareStack integration, copy-paste with Dentrix/Eaglesoft.
  - DentScribe.ai: flat practice-wide fee, quote required.
  — [Denti.AI blog](https://www.denti.ai/blog-posts/best-dental-ai-scribes-in-2026-a-comprehensive-review)
- Overjet and Pearl (imaging-first vendors, also with voice/notes features) do not publish scribe prices. Pearl imaging is listed at "starting at $299/month" on Software Advice, and one cost guide estimates $200-$500/month for Overjet or Pearl. Overjet Voice uses a wireless microphone and a newer free-form perio and hard-tissue charting update (vendor claim). — [Software Advice Pearl](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/370902-Pearl/); [G2 Overjet](https://www.g2.com/products/overjet-ai/pricing); [Henry Schein One/Dentrix Ascend webinar page](https://www.henryscheinone.com/insights/webinars-and-events/elevate-your-practice-with-voice-ai-hands-free-charting-and-notes)
- Insurance narratives and appeals: the ADA itself publishes "The Denial Buster Playbook" ebook on using AI tools like ChatGPT to streamline appeal writing while protecting patient privacy. A vendor guide advises placeholders like [PATIENT NAME], [DOB], [MEMBER ID]. Kiroku and OneChart advertise insurance-narrative or coding features. — [ADA News ebook](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-pubplus/ebooks/the-denial-buster-playbook/); [findskill.ai guide](https://findskill.ai/blog/dental-insurance-preauthorization-letter-ai/)
- AI receptionists: market range for standalone dental AI receptionists is estimated at $200-$800/month by a CloudTalk blog (third-party estimates). Other listings: about $49/month chat and $199/month phone agent (WellnessLiving's CAASI), and $300/month (a Whop listing). Named products include Arini, Dentina AI and Heygent; Arini's pricing is a third-party estimate. I found no funding information. — [CloudTalk Heygent](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/heygent-ai-receptionist-pricing-features-best-alternative/); [WellnessLiving](https://www.wellnessliving.com/blog/virtual-receptionist-pricing-costs-models/); [Rapid Developers](https://www.rapidevelopers.com/ai-agents/ai-receptionist-for-dental-office)
- Smart-speaker precedent: DEXVoice (KaVo Kerr / DEXIS, Alexa-based) lets clinicians capture, show and compare images by voice, e.g. "Alexa, ask My Helper to compare the PA's of number 4." This is imaging-software control, not a knowledge assistant. — [Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/dexvoice-hands-free-software-supporting-the-dexis-imaging-suite-20767859)

### Inferences
- Traction is real for voice perio/scribes (large user counts, PMS marketplace integrations, DSO deployments) and unproven for dental-specific Q&A assistants, which appear to be an open niche. The absence of results is not proof of absence, since the search depth was limited.
- Scribes and voice charting are priced per user or location in a $25-$400/month band; a knowledge assistant bundled into a practice app would be compared against that band.
- Vaporware assessment: I could not verify funding or independent user counts for any product, so "traction" here rests mostly on vendor claims.

### Gaps
- Funding announcements and independent adoption figures were not found.
- No G2/Capterra review content was read (only listing snippets).
- No dental-specific "knowledge assistant" chatbot or Alexa-style assistant product was found; Newo was not found.
- Pricing is dated Feb-Oct 2026 and from aggregator pages; verify with vendors.

## 2. Adoption surveys

### Takeaway
ADA HPI (mid-2026) finds 43.3% of dentists use AI for at least one task, led by imaging/diagnostics (22.8%). Administrative uses are the most accepted; clinical recommendation uses are strongly resisted.

### Cited Findings
- 43.3% of responding dentists use AI for at least one practice task; 26.4% plan to. Sample: 552 private-practice dentists, from HPI's Q2 2026 panel (HPI Perspectives, July 2026). — [ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dentists-ai-usage-and-attitudes)
- Task use: imaging/diagnostics 22.8%; insurance verification 13.6%; explaining clinical findings to patients 13.2%; social media 10.7%; business analytics 10.1%; reception/front desk check-in 10.1%; treatment recommendations under 5%. — [ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dentists-ai-usage-and-attitudes)
- Among non-users who plan to adopt: 34.8% for charting and note-taking, 32.6% for insurance verification, 25.4% for imaging. 82.6% do not plan to use AI for treatment recommendations; 68.3% do not plan to use it to explain findings to patients. — [ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dentists-ai-usage-and-attitudes)
- Quoted dentist concerns: "It has its place but still must be monitored and verified by humans"; "I've tried it with x-ray reading and it was about 30% accurate." — [ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-practice-research/dentists-ai-usage-and-attitudes)
- About 30% (or "about 1 in 3" in another report) of dentists say they never plan to use AI; a separate non-ADA study (dentalreviewed.com, 300 dentists across US/UK/Canada) reports 32% use at least one AI tool. These sources are not directly comparable to the ADA numbers. — [Becker's Dental](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/30-of-dentists-plan-to-never-use-ai-5-things-to-know-about-dentists-ai-integration/); [Becker's Dental 32%](https://www.beckersdental.com/?p=24450)

### Inferences
- The ADA survey does not separately measure general-purpose chatbot use (writing, SOPs) or staff (non-dentist) use; "AI" there is task-framed.

### Gaps
- No staff-level (hygienist, assistant, office manager) adoption survey found; Levin Group / DentistryIQ survey data not retrieved.

## 3. What staff and dentists hand to ChatGPT-style tools

### Takeaway
Documented use cases are writing and admin: patient communications, job ads, SOPs, tone rewrites, insurance appeals. I found no verbatim Reddit/Dentaltown posts or prompts.

### Cited Findings
- Dental-manager guidance lists drafting patient newsletters, blog posts and job ads, writing automated text templates, rewriting messy drafts in a more formal/casual/empathetic tone, and generating step-by-step office procedures (e.g. a PTO-request workflow). — [Dental Managers](https://www.dentalmanagers.com/blog/how-dental-office-managers-can-use-chatgbt/); [Dentaltown blog](https://www.dentaltown.com/blog/post/22060/how-to-grow-your-practice-with-artificial-intelligence-ai)
- A patient (not a dentist) used ChatGPT plus Otter.ai transcripts to contest a $1,200 dental bill; the appeal was denied but she did not pay. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/healthcare/articles/used-ai-dispute-1-200-090501187.html)
- Physician forum (Student Doctor Network) thread on ChatGPT for insurance appeals exists; it is medical, not dental. — [SDN](https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/anyone-using-chatgpt-for-insurance-appeals.1473125/page-2)
- Dentaltown's own articles warn that general tools are not built for healthcare and that sharing patient data puts the practice at risk; they stress a BAA with any AI vendor. — [Dentaltown](https://www.dentaltown.com/magazine/article/9901/getting-ai-right)

### Inferences
- The ADA's own appeal-writing ebook suggests insurance appeal letters are the best-validated high-value writing use.

### Gaps
- Verbatim forum quotes and real prompts from r/Dentistry, r/DentalHygiene, r/DentalAssistant and Dentaltown were not found (search engine did not surface them); likes/dislikes are thus not documented firsthand.
- "What users wish AI could do" (key question 6): no direct evidence found. Weak indirect signal: non-users most want charting/notes (34.8%) and insurance verification (32.6%) per the ADA survey; the Denti.AI review lists gaps in competitors (no note auditing, no intake automation).

## 4. Demand for hands-free voice in the operatory

### Takeaway
Vendors consistently pitch gloved, hands-free charting, and large user counts (Bola, Denti.AI) suggest real demand for perio/charting specifically. Independent evidence on noise, accents and open-bay privacy is absent.

### Cited Findings
- Vendors frame voice as avoiding keyboard/mouse contact while gloved; one headset charting product is described as usable under a face shield and N95. — [Dental Products Report](https://www.dentalproductsreport.com/why-your-mouse-may-soon-be-obselete); [Dentistry Today](https://www.dentistrytoday.com/?p=129795)
- Overjet says earlier systems needed limited fixed commands while its new version accepts free-form speech (vendor claim). — [Henry Schein One page](https://www.henryscheinone.com/insights/webinars-and-events/elevate-your-practice-with-voice-ai-hands-free-charting-and-notes)
- The one controlled voice-command accuracy study found was in surgical robotics (noise-canceling Bluetooth headset, two non-native English speakers, high recognition); a surgical-voice patent notes cross-talk from other staff as a problem. — [arXiv 2409.10225](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10225)

### Inferences
- Obstacles (noise, accents, open-bay cross-talk and privacy) are plausible but not measured in dentistry; headset/close-mic hardware appears to be the common mitigation. A wake-word room speaker in an open bay would face the cross-talk and privacy problems directly.

### Gaps
- No independent dental data on error rates, satisfaction, or open-bay privacy.

## 5. Regulatory and legal boundaries

### Takeaway
FDA CDS guidance excludes only software meeting all four statutory criteria and is HCP-facing only; patient-facing recommendation software is a device. HIPAA requires a BAA before PHI goes to an LLM vendor. A CDT license is required for software vendors, not dentists, with undisclosed royalties.

### Cited Findings
- FDA: the revised final CDS guidance is dated January 29, 2026 (superseding the January 6, 2026 version). Software meeting all four criteria of section 520(o)(1)(E) is excluded from the device definition; one criterion concerns software analyzing images or signals. FDA expanded enforcement discretion for single-output CDS meeting the other criteria. Software aimed at patients or caregivers that gives recommendations is a device; the guidance covers only HCP-facing CDS, announces no new consumer-chatbot policy, and does not address AI. I did not verify the exact criteria wording against the guidance. — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/5-key-takeaways-from-fdas-revised-clinical-decision-support-cds-software-guidance); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-it/insights/publications/2026/01/fda-updates-its-clinical-decision-support-and-general-wellness-guidances-key-points)
- HIPAA: consumer AI products typically lack a BAA, so entering PHI is a disclosure to a third party; a BAA does not settle everything (e.g. psychotherapy notes need authorization). Never use free ChatGPT with patient data (CDA/Abyde). OpenAI reportedly offers BAAs case by case for paying API customers. AAPD (2026) says a CDS tool requires a BAA before any PHI exchange. A compliance attorney flags a staffer pasting patient details into ChatGPT as a potentially reportable breach. — [Holland & Hart](https://www.hollandhart.com/ai-chatbots-and-hipaa-what-healthcare-providers-need-to-know-1); [CDA](https://www.cda.org/newsroom/endorsed-services/ai-in-dentistry-what-are-the-hipaa-violation-risks/); [DrBicuspid](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/legal-issues/podcast/15831225/ai-and-hipaa-in-dentistry-what-your-team-doesnt-know-can-hurt-you); [AAPD](https://www.aapd.org/globalassets/2026-q3-lll.pdf)
- Liability: a dentist who fails to notice a tool's error could still be liable for malpractice (AGD); some policies may not cover AI-tool harm; advice is to ask your carrier. MedPro has chatbot risk resources but no dental-specific policy found. — [AGD (Issuu)](https://issuu.com/agddentist/docs/_12_dec_book/s/61835540); [MedPro](https://www.medpro.com/risks-associated-with-ai-chatbots)
- ADA: SCDI White Paper No. 1106 "Overview of Artificial and Augmented Intelligence Uses in Dentistry" (first published 2022-23 with sources differing on date), with follow-on technical report 1109:2025 and the first US AI standards; guidance, not binding on dentists. It distinguishes augmented intelligence (assists the clinician) from artificial intelligence. — [ADA Standards](https://www.ada.org/resources/practice/dental-standards/informatics-standards/artificial-and-augmented-intelligence); [ADA News 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/what-are-the-standards-for-ai-use-in-dentistry/)
- CDT license: any commercial user who uses, copies or distributes CDT in a product or service needs a commercial license (types: software, published materials, seminars, consultants, benefit plans, TPAs). Codes must be bundled with other assets ("redistribution of the CDT Codes alone is not permitted"); bundling includes using codes to produce records, reports, claims or analytics even if not visible. Fees are royalties under a template agreement and are not published; processing takes 2-4 weeks, sometimes two months; contact CDT-SNODENT@ada.org. Dentists and dental teams do not need a license to use CDT. Open Dental's license is US-distribution only and requires customers to file a compliance form listing end-user sites. The page does not mention AI. — [ADA licensing page](https://www.ada.org/en/publications/ada-store-products/licensing-for-commercial-users); [Open Dental CDT manual](https://opendental.com/manual/cdt.html)

### Inferences
- A knowledge assistant that outputs, suggests or looks up CDT codes (even inside answers) likely falls under the "software" license class; the safest assumption is to apply for a license before shipping CDT-aware features. This is my reading, not ADA guidance.
- Staying outside device status likely means: clinician-facing, transparent about sources so the dentist can independently review, no image/signal analysis, no patient-facing diagnosis or treatment recommendations. Legal counsel should confirm.
- Contracting for a BAA with the LLM vendor (and with practices as business associate) is the practical gate for any feature touching PHI; a knowledge assistant that never receives PHI avoids this.

### Gaps
- State dental board positions on AI: not found.
- HHS OCR AI-specific guidance: only a note that it is forthcoming (CDA); not verified.
- CDT royalty amounts: not published; rough cost unknown.
- No specific malpractice carrier policy on dental AI use found.
