# Proof replaces price as DreamCRM's edge

The 2026 trends that matter most to DreamCRM overturn three assumptions in its September plan. Rivals now publish prices and no-contract wording: Weave lists "starting from $199 per month" and says no long-term contract is required, and Flex Dental sells Open Dental practices a $299 month-to-month plan through a checkout link (facts, vendors' own pages, Nov 23, 2026). The evidence on AI search now favors third-party mentions and review profiles over schema and vendor-written "best" lists. And Google Ads switches AI Max on by default in new Search campaigns while routing offline conversions through an API that needs the click ID's value, which DreamCRM records only as present. For SEO and AI search over the next two quarters, the work is to correct and re-source the comparison pages, then earn presence the site cannot grant itself (G2 and Capterra profiles with real reviews, the Arkansas association's .org, a trade-press byline), with Search Console's and Bing's new AI reports as free scoreboards. For paid, the supportable setup is one Google Search campaign on exact-match competitor and pricing terms with every automation opt-out verified, a small Microsoft mirror, and deferral of Meta, LinkedIn, Reddit and ChatGPT ads; none of it should run before four site gaps close: no Privacy Policy or Terms, a "regularly $500" price nobody has paid, competitor prices the competitors' own pages now contradict, and a domain shared with patient-facing clinic sites. The best 2026 trial benchmark (ChartMogul's survey, published Mar 18, 2026: 8% median free-to-paid, with 4–6% counted as good for a no-card trial) means $1,000 a month buys about eight trials and under one customer a month at the August cost-per-trial bar (estimate), so the budget is a measuring instrument and the Arkansas State Dental Association session on March 11–13, 2027 is the period's real acquisition event. The research could not obtain search volume or click cost for a single target keyword, so every paid projection here rests on generic benchmarks until a Keyword Planner export replaces them.

**How to read the numbers.** Every figure carries one of three labels and a date. **Fact** means the figure sits on a primary source's own page or document (a vendor's price page, a platform help page, a regulation, an organizer's listing) or comes from a survey with a disclosed sample. **Vendor claim** means a commercial party's own data, benchmark or marketing. **Estimate** means a third-party listing, a secondary summary, or this report's own arithmetic and inference. "Nov 23, 2026" means the page was read that day. "The earlier passes" are the Aug 26 and Sep 9, 2026 research recorded in Parts 0–7 and 10.0–10.3 of `docs/marketing-engine.md`. Legal material here is research, not legal advice.

This pass re-tested each against vendors' own pages, platform help pages and 2026 benchmark reports. The strategy's direction survives. Most of the remaining playbook holds. LinkedIn ads still cost $9.39 a click and $202 a lead (vendor claim, Metadata benchmark of 138 advertisers on 2025 data; [Metadata](https://metadata.io/resources/blog/what-linkedin-ads-cost)). Bidding on a rival's name is permitted, and putting it in ad text is the restricted case. llms.txt does nothing. Review-site profiles are infrastructure, referral is the top-rated channel, and human-approved outreach beats autonomous agents. Open Dental's warnings against NexHealth, dated August 2024 and June 2026 and already recorded in September, matter more now that Flex sells to the same practices from a checkout page.

The defensible positioning is narrower, and it reads better as a description than as a superlative. No vendor found combines a published price near $200, a card-free trial, signup without a sales call and integration with more than one practice-management system. Flex comes closest and serves Open Dental only; Weave publishes the price and offers no trial of its full platform, per Part 11 of the engine doc (estimate from about 25 vendor pages read Nov 23, 2026, with Adit, Dental Intelligence and Yapi unreadable). "Only" fails twice: the survey is incomplete, and Weave's own pages now carry a lower headline price and a no-contract line.

## AI search rewards third-party presence, not on-page tricks

### Controlled tests turned against schema, llms.txt and self-ranked lists

The best controlled test available removes schema from the list of AI-citation levers. Ahrefs tracked **1,885 pages that added JSON-LD** between Aug 2025 and Mar 2026 against 4,000 controls across Google AI Overviews, AI Mode and ChatGPT and found no meaningful uplift on any of them (vendor claim, published May 2026, limited to pages that already earned citations; [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)). Google's documentation says the same: "There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add," and its May 2026 guide calls optimizing for generative search "still SEO," with no llms.txt and no content chunking required (facts; [Google AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/)). SE Ranking's ChatGPT study found llms.txt negligible, and found pages with FAQ schema averaging 3.6 citations against 4.2 without (vendor claim, undated; [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/chatgpt-citation-factors/)).

The academic case for "add statistics and quotations" also shrank. The Princeton-led paper's gain of "up to 40%" was measured with five documents fixed in the model's context and no retrieval step. A July 15, 2026 critical survey of 45 studies calls the evidence "narrow" and reports that one benchmark found only 3 of 54 method-and-domain combinations significantly positive (estimate, academic; [critical survey](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.14035.md)).

What correlates with citation sits mostly off the page. Branded web mentions correlate with AI Overview visibility at **0.664, against 0.218 for backlinks** (vendor claim, Ahrefs data on 75,000 brands, seen only in a secondary summary dated May 26, 2026; [Digital Applied](https://digitalapplied.com/blog/entity-seo-knowledge-graph-optimization-guide-2026)). In SE Ranking's 129,000-domain study, referring domains were the strongest correlate of ChatGPT citations, domains with a trust score below 43 averaged about 1.6 citations, and review-platform listings went with roughly 4–6 (vendor claim, undated). A July 2026 snapshot of 278 B2B SaaS prompts found 66% of ChatGPT's brand recommendations made without citing the brand's own site (vendor claim; [Position Digital](https://www.position.digital/blog/chatgpt-ranking-factors/)). For a new, low-authority domain this works against DreamCRM: the weakest lever is the one the site controls.

Vendor-authored "best X" lists are the contested case. They make up 43.8% of the page types AI answers cite on software prompts, and about 35% of cited lists sit on low-authority domains (vendor claim, Ahrefs, Dec 4, 2025; [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/de/blog/best-lists-research/)). Lily Ray's tracking of 100 B2B "best software" queries found that **69% of the 323 cited self-promotional lists came from brands the answer did not recommend**, and she reports an organic demotion that began in January 2026 (estimate, captures of Apr 15, May 15 and Jun 8, 2026; [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/why-calling-yourself-the-best-could)). Peec puts self-promotional lists at about 11% of software-listicle citations (vendor claim, Dec 2025–Feb 2026; [Peec](https://peec.ai/blog/self-promotional-listicles-analysis-from-232k-citations)), and an August 2026 dataset disputes the demotion, reporting a 38% median traffic gain for vendor listicles (estimate, headline only; [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/vendor-listicles-gain-38-median-traffic-since-googles-january-demotion/)). The safe reading: a self-ranked list earns a citation and hands the recommendation to someone else. No controlled test exists for comparison tables, pricing transparency or answer-first structure, so the August claim that a posted price is "structurally ideal" AI material remains a hypothesis. No credible case study shows a new domain winning these queries.

### Google changed the results page and added free AI reporting

Google began its third broad core update of the year on Nov 10, 2026, with a rollout of up to two weeks. No reliable winners-and-losers analysis exists yet, and the practitioner advice is to trust Search Console over rank trackers (fact as reported from Google's statement; [SERoundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-november-2026-core-update-ranking-volatility-42543.html)). FAQ rich results stopped appearing on May 7, 2026 (fact via trade press; [SERoundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-41298.html)). Software-app rich results require an aggregate rating or a review, and self-serving reviews are ineligible for stars, so a product with no third-party reviews cannot honestly earn them (facts, undated Google docs; [Software app](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app); [Review snippet](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)).

AI answers sit on exactly the queries DreamCRM's content targets. Seer Interactive's panel of 5.47 million queries shows AI Overviews on **95.4% of comparison queries**; organic click-through on AI Overview queries fell from 3.19% (Jan 2025) to 1.31% (Dec 2025) and recovered to 2.36% (Feb 2026), and a cited brand kept 2.07% against 0.94% uncited (vendor claim, published Apr 2026; [Seer](https://seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)). A Carnegie Mellon and ISB field experiment measured a 38% cut in organic clicks on triggered queries (estimate, 2026, via [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-cut-organic-clicks-38-field-study-finds/)). Being cited beats being absent, and neither restores the old click-through.

Two free measurement tools arrived. Search Console added dedicated generative-AI performance reports in June 2026, showing impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode by page (fact; [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports)). Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report, in preview since Feb 10, 2026 and extended in June with citation-share and intent views, counts citations in Copilot and partner answers without click data (facts; [Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview)). Paid trackers start at roughly $29–$300 a month, with conflicting price reports (estimate, July 2026 roundups; [Detailed](https://detailed.com/ai-visibility-tools-pricing/)).

The plumbing rules are settled enough to act on. A January 2026 test found that the fetchers behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity read static HTML and do not execute JavaScript (estimate, single controlled test; [Resoneo](https://think.resoneo.com/sentinel/geo-llm-crawler-report.html)). OpenAI separates its search crawler from its training crawler, and Google-Extended gates training only, not Search or AI Overviews (facts, undated docs; [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots); [Google](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers?hl=en)). Bing recommends sitemaps with a true `lastmod` plus IndexNow (fact, July 2025; [Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/July-2025/Keeping-Content-Discoverable-with-Sitemaps-in-AI-Powered-Search)).

### Buyers ask ChatGPT first and rarely click

G2's survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers, fielded in March 2026, has **62% using ChatGPT** (47% a year earlier), 51% starting research in a chatbot more often than in Google, and 45% naming a review-site citation as the most confidence-inspiring signal in an AI answer. Among small-business buyers the split is roughly ChatGPT 58, Gemini 21, Claude 7, Copilot 6, Perplexity 2 (vendor claim; G2 owns the review sites it finds persuasive; [G2](https://learn.g2.com/g2-2026-ai-search-insight-report?tp=1)). Traffic is another matter: AI engines send under 2% of B2B search referral sessions (vendor claim, Octane11, Q2 2026; [Octane11](https://octane11.com/insights/insights-state-of-b2b-ai-search-q2-26/)). Buyers also check what they are told: 72% always or very often fact-check AI output, up from 58% a year earlier (fact, TrustRadius survey of 1,862 buyers, Jan 2026; [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/trustradius-ai-has-changed-how-buyers-research-but-not-what-they-trust/53853/)). No buyer data specific to dental or healthcare practices was found. The working assumption is influence without clicks, visible later as branded search.

### DreamCRM surfaced in none of ten buyer queries

A field check on Nov 23, 2026 ran ten buyer queries through a web-search tool, not logged-out Google results, so positions are unverified. Review aggregators (Capterra, Software Advice, GetApp, G2, TrustRadius), vendor blogs (NexHealth, Weave, Emitrr, Curve) and listicle farms filled the results, and dreamcreatestudio.com appeared in none ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/dental-software/)). Vendor blogs do rank for "best dental patient communication software," which shows a vendor-written page can compete, though the authority of those domains was not measured. The brand name collides with an iOS app and at least two unrelated CRM products called DreamCRM or Dreams CRM ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/dreamcrm/id6464315192); [GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/software/dreams-crm)). The domain's index count and authority were not established.

### What this means for the SEO and AI-search work

Accuracy comes before scale. The comparison pages are the site's core search asset and the pages paid traffic lands on, and two of them trail the competitor's own published numbers (detailed in the claims section). Their structure already matches what the evidence calls credible, since each leads with what the rival does better. The fix is sourcing: cite the vendor's own page with a retrieval date, write "does not publish pricing" where that is true, and add Flex as the honest alternative for Open Dental practices. A list that ranks DreamCRM first stays ruled out, as the program's own Law 6 already says.

Effort then moves from markup to presence. Organization, Breadcrumb and Article markup stay for eligibility; FAQ and software-app markup earn nothing until real reviews exist. The hours belong to the channels that correlate with citation: a claimed G2 and Capterra profile with first reviews, a member-benefit page on the Arkansas association's .org, one non-promotional contributed article in Dental Economics, whose guidelines (updated Oct 23, 2026) let editors strip brand names and links ([Dental Economics](https://www.dentaleconomics.com/submission-guidelines)), and human participation where dentists talk. The brand should always travel with the word "dental" to separate it from its namesakes.

Measurement can start free. The session's site audit (`docs/research/marketing-engine-2026-11/site-audit.md`) confirms the pages are server-rendered and that robots.txt admits AI crawlers, so what remains is connecting Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submitting the sitemap with honest `lastmod` values and IndexNow, and recording a baseline. That baseline must separate marketing pages from clinic sites, because the audit found clinic sites also resolve under `www.dreamcreatestudio.com/site/…` and declare their canonical address on the marketing domain. The September design ran about 40 prompts weekly across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and AI Overviews. G2's small-business split argues for ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode, monthly, with Perplexity dropped (estimate from the shares above). Judgment on the November core update waits until early December and is read in Search Console.

The phrase "regularly $500" also sits in the homepage and pricing-page meta descriptions, the text a search snippet or an AI answer lifts first.

## Paid search now defaults to automation a small budget must switch off

### Google: exact match takes explicit opt-outs

Google's help page states that "New Search campaigns have the **AI Max feature turned on by default**" (fact, undated page read Nov 23, 2026; [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15909989?hl=en)). AI Max adds keywordless search-term matching, text customization and final-URL expansion. On Sept 1, 2026 Google also auto-upgraded existing campaigns that used campaign-level broad match or automatically created assets (fact as reported from Google's email; [SERoundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-ads-migrate-ai-max-sep1-41829.html)). Exact match stops behaving as exact once AI Max is on: one practitioner analysis found **more than 25% of impressions attributed to exact-match keywords arrived through AI Max matching**, and one account saw competitor searches take 69% of impressions (vendor claim, 2026; [SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/google-ads-ai-max-is-making-more-decisions-for-you-heres-what-to-audit-now/596972)). The opt-out exists at campaign level under Settings, and the parts switch off separately: search-term matching per ad group, text customization and final-URL expansion in the asset-optimization panel. The August ruling to run "manual/exact-match" stands, and executing it now takes verified toggles at campaign creation; choosing the exact match type no longer delivers it.

Bidding has fewer manual options than the August plan assumed. Enhanced CPC left Search the week of Mar 31, 2025 (fact via Google's bidding FAQ; [Google](https://support.google.com/google-ads/faq/10286469)). The practitioner path at about $30 a day is Manual CPC or Maximize Clicks with a bid cap, and this research could not confirm from a Google page that Manual CPC is selectable in the late-2026 campaign wizard. The one numeric floor Google publishes is 15 conversions in 30 days for Target ROAS (fact, undated page; [Google](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637?hl=en)). Google is replacing the Search Partners and Display on-off switches with "channel prioritization" and retires the existing Display spend allocation in February 2027 (estimate, trade press, 2026; [SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/google-ads-introduces-channel-prioritization-and-budget-allocation/592833)), so network settings need checking in the interface at launch and again in February. Existing Search campaigns are automatically eligible to serve inside AI Mode, in the same auction, with no separate opt-out (fact, Google blog, Oct 2026; [Google](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ads-in-ai-mode-global/)).

Policy is workable. Google "will not restrict" trademarks used as keywords. It restricts a trademark in the text of "an ad from a direct competitor," acts on complaints against specific advertisers, and applies a restriction to ads that share the same second-level domain (facts, undated policy page; [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6118?hl=en)). Rival names therefore stay out of headlines, descriptions, paths and sitelinks, and the keyword does the matching. The healthcare policy does not mention B2B software and expressly leaves dental-only insurance ads unrestricted; "Health" does appear among the personalized-advertising categories, which is the item to check before building any remarketing list ([Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en); [Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465?hl=en)). A secondary source reports that Google clarified in July 2025 that the health-interest restriction does not apply to brands marketing to licensed healthcare professionals (estimate; [Accelerated Digital Media](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/?p=8226)). The misrepresentation policy bars "failure to clearly and conspicuously disclose the payment model or full expense" and promotions that do not match the destination ([Misrepresentation](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?hl=en)). Advertiser verification can be demanded at any time with a deadline that "can't be extended"; review takes up to five business days and, rarely, 30, and it checks identity or registration documents against the payments profile (facts, undated pages; [verification timelines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15588490)).

### Measurement needs the click ID's value, not its presence

Since **June 15, 2026**, offline conversion imports and enhanced conversions for leads run through the Data Manager API. Google's page says the click ID is "required if you're not using a tag to collect user-provided data," and its troubleshooting page says Google keeps a click ID for 90 days (facts, undated pages; [enhanced conversions for leads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15713840?hl=en); [offline import errors](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13321563?hl=en)). This suits DreamCRM's first-party posture better than a tag would: the site can stay free of Google scripts and still report "trial started" and "became paying" server to server. It cannot do so today, because its attribution records only that a click ID was present. Storing the value at landing and carrying it onto the signup record is the one engineering prerequisite for the campaign, and the Privacy Policy has to describe the upload. Consent Mode is required for visitors in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, so a US-only campaign with presence-based location targeting does not trigger it (fact per Google's consent pages, via search summary; [Google](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13695607)). The API's developer guide could not be read, so request shapes and event-age limits remain unverified.

### Rivals advertise demos and gift cards; none sampled leads with a price

Google's Ads Transparency Center shows about 800 ads for getweave.com, about 700 for nexhealth.com, about 200 for revenuewell.com, 91 for dentalintel.com and 75 for adit.com (facts as observed Nov 23, 2026; the counts span all formats and include client-practice ads, so they are a rough proxy for spend; [Transparency Center](https://adstransparency.google.com/?region=US&domain=getweave.com)). Weave's text ad offers a "$150 Amazon gift card with Weave demo." NexHealth runs "NexHealth For Eaglesoft - Writes Back To Your Schedule" with "See pricing" in the copy, while its pricing page ends in a quote form. RevenueWell targets specialties with "Periodontic Website Design" ([NexHealth ads](https://adstransparency.google.com/?region=US&domain=nexhealth.com); [RevenueWell ads](https://adstransparency.google.com/?region=US&domain=revenuewell.com)). In a thin sample of up to eight creatives per domain, no ad named a rival and none put a number in a headline. A headline that states the price, the card-free trial and the absence of a demo was unoccupied ground in that sample (estimate). Whether rivals bid on each other's names stayed unknown, because the tool shows ads and not keywords.

### At $1,000 a month the campaign measures; it cannot scale

No keyword-level cost or volume could be obtained, so the arithmetic below uses generic benchmarks: a $5.42 all-industry median US search CPC and a median of $8.50–$14.00 for non-brand B2B SaaS (vendor claims, 2026; [GrowthSpree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/saas-google-ads-benchmarks-2026-cpc-cpl-ctr-conversion-rate-by-vertical.md); [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/google-ads-benchmarks-2026)). The click-to-trial rate of 3–8% is a planning assumption in the research notes, not a measured figure. Every cell is an estimate.

| Scenario | Clicks per $1,000 | Trials per month | Cost per trial | Customers per month at 8% trial-to-paid | Cost per customer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $5.00 CPC, 8% click-to-trial | 200 | 16 | $63 | 1.3 | $780 |
| $5.00 CPC, 3% click-to-trial | 200 | 6 | $167 | 0.5 | $2,080 |
| $8.50 CPC, 8% click-to-trial | 118 | 9 | $106 | 0.8 | $1,330 |
| $8.50 CPC, 3% click-to-trial | 118 | 4 | $283 | 0.3 | $3,540 |
| $14.00 CPC, 8% click-to-trial | 71 | 6 | $175 | 0.5 | $2,190 |
| $14.00 CPC, 3% click-to-trial | 71 | 2 | $467 | 0.2 | $5,830 |

Two of six scenarios meet the August bar of under $120 per trial, and only those two land under the $1,600 ceiling on cost per customer. The bar has lost its margin: **$120 per trial equals $1,600 per customer only if 7.5% of trials pay**, and ChartMogul's median is 8% with 4–6% counted as good. Volume is the larger constraint. At the bar, $1,000 buys about eight trials a month, about 50 over two quarters and about four customers at 8%. Four conversions out of 50 carries a 95% confidence interval of roughly 3% to 19%, wide enough to hold both a failing business and an excellent one (this report's arithmetic). The master dial rule from August, which cuts spend when blended cost per customer exceeds $1,600 for two consecutive months, will read noise when a month produces zero, one or two customers. Smart bidding stays out of reach for the same reason. The campaign's honest job is to produce search-term data, a measured click-to-trial rate and message tests. Trial-to-paid has to be measured on trials pooled from every source.

Demand is the other unknown. The research's only read on volume for terms such as "weave alternative" and "nexhealth pricing" is an unverified guess of tens to low hundreds of searches a month, which means $1,000 could exceed the exact-match demand available (estimate).

## Self-serve benchmarks cut the trial planning number to 8% or less

### The best 2026 benchmark sits at the bottom of the September range

ChartMogul surveyed 200 B2B software products in January 2026 (typical respondent $1–10 million in annual recurring revenue) and published on Mar 18, 2026. Median free-to-paid conversion is **8%**. One in five trial products requires a card, and those convert at 30%, "more than 5x" the no-card rate. Its bands are 4–6% good and 10–15% great for no-card trials, and 25–35% and 50–60% for card-required trials (fact; [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report-2/)). Higher figures circulate without verifiable methods, including 25.2% for no-card trials in one self-published study (vendor claim, 2026; [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026)). The planning number is 8%, with 5% a live possibility.

One inference in the underlying research notes should not be carried forward. The notes say ChartMogul's funnel shows no-card trials winning on paying customers per visitor. The illustrative funnel extracted from the same report points the other way: per 1,000 visitors, about 45 no-card signups yield about 4 customers and about 35 card-required signups yield about 11 (estimate, read from a graphic, and inconsistent with the report's own "more than 5x" text; the September research file recorded the same graphic as 3.6 and 10.5). ChartMogul's text says only that the gap between freemium and trials disappears after signup rates, and that a card wall can reduce total customers unless the product justifies the friction. The case for keeping DreamCRM's trial card-free rests elsewhere: no automatic charge means no negative-option exposure, every sampled rival ad leads to a demo or a quote, and current traffic cannot power an A/B test of the question. It is an experiment for a later traffic level, not a settled finding.

### Seven days is a product risk the benchmarks do not measure

Fourteen days is the market default, used by 62% of products (fact, ChartMogul, Mar 2026). No controlled 2024–26 test of 7 against 14 against 30 days exists for low-priced B2B software. A 2023 randomized experiment in a freemium setting found 7-day trials slightly ahead (estimate, secondary; [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-length/)). Nothing shows that seven days converts worse in general. DreamCRM's exposure is specific: a busy owner must connect a practice-management system inside the window. Starting the clock at first value, or granting one human extension, are cheap designs that no source has tested.

Evidence for concierge onboarding is anecdotal. One vendor reports 14 calls and 8 conversions from an onboarding call offered during trials (vendor claim, undated; [Customer.io](https://customer.io/blog/concierge-onboarding-doubled-conversions)), so the founder's setup call has to be measured on DreamCRM's own cohort, with the self-selection noted. Generic activation benchmarks, about 37.5% on average (vendor claim, Userpilot, 2025; [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/activation-rate-benchmark/)), describe products with no integration step and do not transfer. No 2025–26 data exists on what a grader or calculator converts, so the practice grader's yield is another in-house measurement. Lifecycle email should trigger on product events and replies, because opens no longer measure reading: Gmail began rolling out Gemini summaries in January 2026 and open tracking is unreliable (fact for the rollout; [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gmail/gmail-is-entering-the-gemini-era/)).

### Referral tops every channel survey while open affiliate programs fail

Kyle Poyar's survey of 300 software go-to-market leaders, fielded Sept 2026 and published Oct 14, ranks referral and network introductions first, a core channel for **62%**. SEO is core for 51% and founder or employee LinkedIn posting for 50%. Cold outreach is "officially oversaturated," AI sales agents are "not living up to early hype," and among companies with deals under $5,000, 35% use product-led growth as a primary motion, while founder-led sales is the top motion below $1 million in revenue at 44% (fact, survey; [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2026-state-of-b2b-gtm-report)). Open affiliate programs perform differently: across 2,847 programs, 7.6% of affiliates ever refer anyone, 1.28% generate a sale and 15.6% of programs survive long term (vendor claim, Rewardful, updated July 21, 2026; [Rewardful](https://rewardful.com/articles/state-of-saas-affiliate-programs-report)).

The dental-specific signals agree, though both are thin. A bootstrapped dental-software founder reports "all of our sales are inbound," mostly word of mouth (estimate, interview; [FounderPath](https://founderpath.com/resources/rohit-garg-practice-by-numbers-bootstrapped-dental-software/watch)), and Jane App's co-founder named professional-association events as its primary paid channel (estimate, 2018 interview; [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/janeapp/interviews/janeapp-alison-taylor-2018)). The supported design is a short list of named partners in place of a public affiliate page. The Arkansas association relationship is the channel type the survey ranks first.

### Buyers verify, and a published BAA is table stakes

Verification is the 2026 buyer habit. Beyond the 72% who fact-check AI output, 58% click through when an AI answer cites a vendor and only 42% say the citation builds trust (fact, Informa TechTarget survey of 499 buyers, 2026; [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/marketing-tech/ai-citations-earn-clicks-from-58-of-tech-buyers-and-trust-from-only-42)). In dental software the Business Associate Agreement is published as a matter of course: Open Dental and Dental Intelligence post theirs, and the ADA publishes a sample for practices ([Open Dental](https://www.opendental.com/site/hipaaopendental.html); [Dental Intelligence](https://dentalintel.com/baa-subcontractor); [ADA](https://www.ada.org/en/resources/practice/legal-and-regulatory/faqs-on-hipaa-business-associates)). Its absence is the risk; its presence is not a differentiator. Weave's own survey has 60% of practices concerned about data privacy with AI (vendor claim, Aug 20, 2026; [Weave newsroom](https://www.getweave.com/newsroom/)). No source measured a conversion lift from trust pages, guarantees, founding-rate offers or price locks. The only numbers are low-quality vendor blogs.

## Four site gaps stand between DreamCRM and a compliant launch

### No Privacy Policy or Terms

The site collects email addresses through its grader, partner form and signup and posts neither a Privacy Policy nor Terms. California's online privacy statute requires an operator that collects personal information from California consumers to "conspicuously post its privacy policy," with a violation arising only if the operator fails to post within 30 days of being notified. Its definition of "consumer" covers personal and household purchases, so its fit to a B2B list is arguable (facts, statute text via summaries; [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 22575](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-8/chapter-22/section-22575/); [22577](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_22577)). The ad platforms make the argument moot: Google Ads requires advertisers using data segments to disclose that use in a privacy policy and requires a policy link on lead-form assets (fact, undated page; [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2549063)). Twenty-four states now have comprehensive privacy laws (estimate, tracker dated Oct 28, 2026; [MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/10/28/twenty-four-states-now-have-comprehensive-consumer-privacy-laws)). Most apply above thresholds such as 100,000 or 35,000 consumers of a state, which a company of DreamCRM's size is unlikely to meet (estimate, law-firm summaries, 2026; [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/new-in-2026-state-privacy-laws-in-indiana-kentucky-and-rhode-island/)). The policy has to describe the real data flows, because a posted policy that does not match practice is its own violation ([22576](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_22576)).

Terms should state price, monthly renewal and the cancellation path before purchase. A card-free trial that charges nothing unless the practice subscribes sits outside the "free-to-pay conversion" structure that federal and California auto-renewal laws regulate, and those laws protect consumers (facts and secondary summaries; [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 17601](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_bus_and_prof_code_section_17601); [15 U.S.C. 8403](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/8403)). New York's renewal statute for business service contracts excludes renewal periods of a month or less, which makes the annual plan on the pricing page the one for counsel to check (estimate, secondary; [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/ja/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2014/05/the-enforceability-of-automatic-renewal-provisions-under-new-york-law)). The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated on July 8, 2025 and rulemaking restarted in March 2026, with no re-proposed rule found (facts via law-firm alerts; [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/ftc-launches-new-rulemaking-on-the-negative-option-rule)).

### "Regularly $500" is the highest-risk sentence on the site

The FTC's pricing guide says a former price must be "the actual, bona fide price at which the article was offered to the public on a regular basis for a reasonably substantial period of time." A price never offered is its textbook fictitious comparison (fact; [16 CFR 233.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/233.1)). California bars advertising a former price unless it prevailed within the preceding three months or the ad states when it did (fact; [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 17501](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17501)); whether that statute protects business buyers was not confirmed. **No customer has been charged $500.** Google's ads policy separately bars unclear pricing, and a competitor or a prospect can ask who ever paid the higher number.

Three wordings are defensible. The first is "$200 a month, one plan, no contract," with no comparison. The second is a future list price stated as future, with a date and a documented decision to implement it. The third is a founding rate for a fixed number of practices, which works only if the cap is real, because the same guide warns against a "limited" offer that "in fact, is not limited" ([16 CFR 233.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/233.5)). No enforcement action or study specific to founding-rate pricing in software was found.

### Rival prices need the rival's own dated page

The Lanham Act creates liability for misrepresenting "another person's goods, services, or commercial activities" in commercial advertising, while FTC policy encourages naming competitors so long as the comparison is clear (facts; [15 U.S.C. 1125](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1125); [16 CFR 14.15](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/14.15)). A January 2026 ruling let a restaurant-software vendor's false-advertising claims against a rival proceed (estimate, secondary; [Bloomberg](https://news.bgov.com/ip-law/chownows-false-advertising-claims-against-rival-move-forward)). No dispute specific to software comparison pages with price tables was found.

The live Weave page still reports \~$249–$449 a month and annual agreements, per the session's site audit; a version sourced to Weave's own pages was drafted during this session and is not yet live. The Solutionreach page reports about $329 a month against that vendor's own "Pricing starts at $199." The other pages cite "third-party reviews (2025–2026)" with no named source. The stronger page quotes the vendor: the published floor, the bundle prices, and the dated terms-of-service clauses on price increases and renewals. Where a vendor publishes nothing, as with NexHealth, Podium and RevenueWell, "does not publish pricing; ask for a quote" is accurate, and any third-party figure needs a named source and a retrieval date. Plain-text use of a rival's name to describe a comparison is ordinary practice; logos and implied affiliation are not (estimate; no controlling authority was verified).

Absolute claims carry the same exposure. The product page's "a shop and membership plans nobody else ships," flagged in the site audit, sits beside Clerri, which markets itself as the leading dental membership-plan software and claims 25,000-plus dentists (vendor claim, Nov 23, 2026; [Clerri](https://www.clerri.com)). Copy that names Open Dental as reached through sanctioned paths sits beside Open Dental's own statement above.

### Ad tags and patient pages share one domain

Clinic websites and patient portals live on subdomains of the marketing site's domain, and the site audit found clinic sites also resolve under the www host itself. DreamCRM is a business associate for what happens on those pages. Three findings make that architecture the central tracking constraint. The Dental Intelligence settlement shows the litigation exposure of a dental vendor accused of sharing patient information with Google. California's SB 690, signed Sept 30, 2026, removed private lawsuits only under the pen-register provision; wiretap claims under Section 631 survive, though courts have raised the standing bar (estimates, law-firm alerts; [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/ru/pubs/2026/10/california-eliminates-private-right-of-action-under-cipas-pen-register-and-trap-and-trace-provision); [WLF](https://www.wlf.org/2026/07/28/publishing/post-popa-v-microsoft-courts-in-california-keep-the-standing-bar-high-for-web-tracking-claims/)). And the platforms act at domain level: Meta classifies a data source by domain, and Google applies trademark restrictions by second-level domain.

The current first-party-only design is the lowest-risk posture found. If an ad platform's tag is ever added, it loads on marketing routes only, scoped by route and not by host, and never on a clinic site or portal. The server-side click-ID upload avoids the question for Google. Visitor-identification tools stay out; a March 2026 ruling let a California privacy claim over tracking proceed (estimate, law-firm commentary; [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/06/courts-expand-ccpas-private-right-of-action/)). The prospect database does not make DreamCRM a data broker under California's Delete Act while nothing is sold or licensed, since the definition requires selling (estimate, law-firm summary; [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260115-think-you-re-not-a-data-broker-calprivacy-regulations)). HHS's Security Rule overhaul slipped to a July 2027 target, leaving the current rule in force (fact via trade press, 2026; [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/feds-push-back-hipaa-security-rule-overhaul-july-2027)).

### AI and HIPAA claims need evidence on file

FTC enforcement on AI claims has moved into business-to-business marketing. **Seven of its last eight AI-washing cases** involved claims made to businesses, including a May 21, 2026 action over an AI marketing tool sold to small businesses (estimate, law-firm summary; [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/05/ftc-ai-washing-action-underscores-enforcement-in-business-to-business-context)). An advertiser must "possess and rely upon adequate substantiation" for performance claims, and testimonials are not that evidence (fact; [16 CFR 255.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2)). "Drafts messages for your team to approve" is a description. "Fills chairs" is a results claim that needs tracked bookings behind it or softer wording.

On HIPAA the site's silence is the right default. HHS does not certify compliance, and the FTC's $250,000 settlement with Henry Schein Practice Solutions in 2016 punished a dental-software vendor for overstating its encryption (facts; [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2003/are-we-required-to-certify-our-organizations-compliance-with-the-standards/); [FTC](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/05/ftc-approves-final-order-henry-schein-practice-solutions-case)). The legal duty runs separately from the marketing: a practice may disclose patient information to a business associate only under a written agreement (fact; [45 CFR 164.502](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.502)). "We sign a BAA with every practice" is the sentence buyers expect, and it belongs on the site once it is true end to end.

### Email, reviews and partner rules

Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft enforce bulk-sender rules at 5,000 messages a day: aligned SPF, DKIM and DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, and spam complaints under 0.3% (estimate, vendor summaries of the mailbox providers' rules, 2025–26; [Red Sift](https://redsift.com/guides/bulk-email-sender-requirements)). An opt-in nurture list sits far below the threshold and should meet the standard anyway. The FTC's reviews rule bars compensation "conditioned expressly or by implication" on a review's sentiment; the first warning letters went out Dec 22, 2025, with penalties up to $53,088 per violation, and the rule does not say whether it reaches business reviews on G2 or Capterra (facts and secondary; [16 CFR 465.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.4); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new)). The safe program asks every customer, pays the same nominal amount regardless of rating and runs through the platform's own labelled incentive. A partner's commission is a material connection that the partner must disclose and DreamCRM must police ([16 CFR 255.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5)). On texting, sources conflict on whether the FCC's revised consent-revocation rules took effect Nov 25, 2026 or the revoke-all rule now sits at Jan 31, 2027; cold texting stays prohibited either way ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/fcc-revises-tcpa-revocation-of-consent-rules-that-were-set-to-go-into-effect-in-january/)).

## Conclusion

The September plan treated transparency as the moat. The market's largest vendor now publishes a starting price a dollar below DreamCRM's and says it requires no long-term contract, so the moat is whatever a buyer, an ad reviewer or an AI agent can verify. DreamCRM's site fails that test in three places today: a list price nobody paid, rival prices the rivals' own pages contradict, and practice-management coverage that one of those systems publicly disputes. A single accuracy pass pays four ways. It removes the largest legal exposures, satisfies the ad platforms' prerequisites, strengthens the pages AI answers quote, and restores the honesty the brand sells. It costs attention and nothing else, which makes it the highest-return work of the two quarters.

The second shift concerns what money can prove. Six thousand dollars of search spend yields about 50 trials if the August cost-per-trial bar is met (estimate): enough to learn what a click costs and which message converts, not enough to learn whether trials become customers. Through May 2027 the binding constraint is evidence, not budget: a trial-to-paid rate measured on pooled cohorts, real reviews on third-party sites, and a room of Arkansas dentists hearing the pitch through their own association. Three findings would change the plan: a Keyword Planner export showing demand too thin for the budget, a measured trial-to-paid rate under 5%, or a Weave packaging change under its new owner.
